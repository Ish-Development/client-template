# The four jobs

Studio method. Same for every client. Do not change per client.

We do not organise a site by subject. We organise it by the job each page
does for the business. A job gives us something to measure and improve.

## Converter
Turns interest into a conversation. A form, a contact section, a booking
calendar. Every other job exists to move a buyer toward a Converter.

## Collector
Finds strangers and keeps them with an email. Newsletter signup, a guide or
case study traded for an email. Catches people who are not ready yet.

## Attractor
Catches someone who was not looking. Campaign pages, launches, events,
announcements. Relies on social. Spikes and fades, and that is the job.
Works with Collectors: catch someone new, then keep their information.

## Informer
The bulk of the site. Answers what a buyer must settle before choosing:
what it does, who is behind it, whether it works for companies like theirs.
Handles questions and objections so sales can focus on closing.

## Rules
- One page, one job. Every page except the home page has exactly one job
  and one next action.
- Measure a page by the share of visitors who took that next action.
- Pages and sections both have jobs. A section can do a different job from its page.
  Example: an event page is an Attractor. The signup section on it is a Collector.
- One main goal for the whole site, two at most. Something the business
  would celebrate: qualified leads reaching sales, or revenue.
  Page views, time on site, and sessions explain performance. They are not performance.
- Which page does which job is settled with the client in the workshop.

## How jobs relate to the buyer journey
The journey (agent/journey.yaml) is workshop input. Jobs are what we design with.

| Journey stage | Jobs that serve it |
|---|---|
| Awareness | Attractor, Collector |
| Education | Informer, Collector |
| Selection | Informer, Converter |
| Commit | Converter |
